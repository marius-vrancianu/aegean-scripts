## Result: outcome A — NOT SUPPORTED (as predicted)

M1 cannot tell the Hebrew verb lexicon from a lexicon of no language on Linear A sign-groups.

| | Real Hebrew | Matched pseudo-lexicons (95%) | Excess | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Test 1**, Linear A, 414 sign-groups | 0.775 | 0.769 (0.734–0.800) | +0.006 | **p1 = 0.397** |
| Greek control, 902 words | 0.532 | 0.512 (0.486–0.538) | +0.020 | 0.064 |
| Greek control at Linear A's lengths | 0.684 | 0.660 | +0.024 | |
| **Test 2**, Linear A minus control | | (−0.032 to +0.035) | **−0.017** | **p2 = 0.840** |

- p for the pair: 0.840. Both p1 and p2 are above α = 0.05, so the outcome is A on the raw values.
- **In words.** Of 414 Linear A sign-groups, the method finds a Hebrew verb root for 321 (77.5%). With a random lexicon of the same size it finds a "root" for 76.9% on average: about three sign-groups fewer. 397 of 1,000 random lexicons did at least as well as Hebrew.
- The Hebrew lexicon's small advantage over random lexicons is larger on Greek words (+0.024) than on Linear A (+0.006). The difference has the wrong sign for the hypothesis.

## Power, as measured
- The pseudo-lexicons' hit rates on Linear A have a standard deviation of 0.017 (the hypothesis guessed "of the order of 0.013"). Test 1 would have needed an excess of about +0.03 (some 1.6 standard deviations; the upper end of the 95% range is 0.800 against a mean of 0.769), that is about twelve more matched sign-groups. The observed excess is +0.006.
- Test 2 would have needed a difference of about +0.03; the observed difference is −0.017.
- Tier 1 has no known positive. This outcome says the method is uninformative for this lexicon. It does not say that Linear A is not Semitic.

## Descriptive results (none of them decides anything)
- **Per source:** SigLA 0.782 against 0.777 (n = 321); lineara.xyz 0.772 against 0.765 (n = 400); Younger 0.754 against 0.747 (n = 195). The same picture in each.
- **By length** (real against pseudo-lexicons): 2 signs 0.963 / 0.933; 3 signs 0.839 / 0.825; 4 signs 0.619 / 0.648; 5 or more 0.396 / 0.409. At two signs almost anything matches; at four and more the real lexicon is slightly below the pseudo-lexicons.
- **The Finding 001 null** (not size-matched): 0.803 (0.773–0.831), so real Hebrew is 0.028 below it (p = 0.962). On the control it is 0.029 below. This reproduces the pattern of Finding 001, where real Hebrew scored below every random lexicon, and shows that the pattern came from the null and not from Hebrew: the same deficit appears on Greek words.
- **Lexicon size:** pseudo-lexicons of 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 times the real size match 0.678, 0.808, 0.896 and 0.925 of the sign-groups.
- **Random sign strings:** real sign-groups 0.775, random strings of the same lengths and sign frequencies 0.700 (0.669–0.727). Real sign-groups are easier to match than random strings whatever the lexicon, as in Finding 001's addendum.
- **Degrees of freedom:** a sign-group has 7.3 readings on average (median 9.5, maximum 41); the expanded lexicon covers 4.2% of the possible forms.
