## Points from the review of PR #60 (`docs/reviews/H-A5-06-review.md`), seen before the run
- **N1, Test 2 and class counts.** Test 2 re-weights by number of signs, but M2's hit rate depends on the number of consonant classes, and Linear A groups have more classes per sign than the Greek words (among three-sign groups, 26% of Linear A's have two classes against 41% of the control's). A **post hoc, unregistered** re-weighting by sign count × class count (`experiments/A5.17/m2_class_reweighted_test2.py`, output `results/M2-class-reweighted-test2.json`; same pseudo-lexicons) gives difference and p: Hebrew −0.030, 0.921; English −0.031, 0.924; Turkish +0.000, 0.513. It decides nothing and changes no reading.
- **N2, groups that cannot match.** At threshold 1/4 a two-class skeleton matches only two-class forms. Hebrew has none, so its 130 two-class groups can never match (81 of the 83 two-sign groups among them), nor can the groups of six or more classes; with English and Turkish, the few groups of six or more classes can never match. Dropping groups that can never match changes no p1: they are misses under the real lexicon and under every pseudo-lexicon alike, so every hit rate is scaled by the same factor and the ranks do not change (review of PR #62, N4).
- **N4, M2's Test 1 is conservative.** On uniform random strings every real lexicon trails its random lexicons by 0.02–0.04 (`experiments/A5.17/m2_threshold_calibration.py`), mostly at three and four consonant classes. An outcome A under M2 therefore says less than one under M1.
- **N5.** The threshold calibration is now committed with its seeds (`experiments/A5.17/m2_threshold_calibration.py`) and reproduces the quoted figures exactly.
- **N6.** "The strictest threshold that allows any edit" holds when both strings have at most four classes, as the lexicon forms do.
- **Correction (review of PR #62, N3).** The saturation figure quoted for threshold 1/3 in H-A5-06 to H-A5-08 and in research-notes ("0.93–0.99 of two- and three-class skeletons") is English's. Across the three lexicons it is 0.90–0.99 (Turkish two-class strings 0.90). The calibration script was committed after the fact; it reproduces the quoted figures, which is all that can be checked.
- **Hebrew null overlap (review of PR #60, N3; review of PR #62, N5).** The length-stratified pseudo-lexicons share on average 268 of the real lexicon's 414 three-class forms, so for Hebrew the null is close to the lexicon itself at three classes, and the test has little room.
- **N7.** M2's permissiveness figures count strings longer than any lexicon form, so they are not comparable with M1's.

## What follows (all three M2 pairs)
- **M2 is uninformative for all three lexicons**, as M1 was. With M2 no lexicon is even above its random lexicons on Linear A.
- **The Greek-word advantage (descriptive, my reading, rule 5).** Under M2 the unrelated lexicons' advantage on Greek words disappears for Turkish (+0.032 → −0.003) and shrinks for English (+0.033 → +0.019; at Linear A's lengths +0.028 → +0.022). The change between methods is not tested. M2 differs from M1 in its licences, its classes, its lexicon conversions and its matching rule, so this does not say which of them carried the M1 advantage. English, the Indo-European lexicon, is the one that keeps part of it. The bigram-matched null of the next protocol revision (H-A5-09) tests one candidate cause, letter co-occurrence.
- **Limits:** M2's Test 1 is conservative (review N4), and for Hebrew part of the input can never match (N2). An A under M2 is weaker evidence of uninformativeness than an A under M1.
