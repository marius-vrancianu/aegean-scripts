## Part 1 — Protocol (revision 2)

**Everything in H-A5-03, Part 1 holds without change, except the decision null, the batch and the closure of H-A5-03.** That includes H-A5-01's terms, data, both tests, length bins, α = 0.05, the decision rule with the batch correction, the deviations rule, the reporting, every seed, and H-A5-03's rule for pairs run under several protocols (point 4), extended below.

### What changes, and why
1. **Decision null: bigram-matched, length-stratified pseudo-lexicons** (`nulls.bigram_matched_lexicon`). As H-A5-03's null, but a form's letters are drawn from a first-order Markov chain fitted to the real lexicon: the first letter from the real first-letter frequencies at that length, each later letter from the real frequencies of the letters that follow the previous one (pooled over lengths and positions). A draw that ends early (a letter never followed by anything) is discarded. K = 1,000, drawn in sequence from `random.Random(20261114)`.
   - *Why:* under H-A5-03, both unrelated lexicons beat length-matched noise on Greek words with M1 (English +0.033, Turkish +0.032, p ≈ 0.015; descriptive). One candidate cause is letter co-occurrence inside forms, which H-A5-03's null does not match (its letters are independent given position). Another is something specific to M1 (its licences, classes, lexicon conversion or matching rule): under M2 the advantage is gone for Turkish (−0.003) and smaller for English (+0.019, p = 0.139), a change that was not tested. This revision addresses the first candidate only.
   - *What it does not match:* co-occurrence beyond adjacent letters, and position-specific transitions.
2. **Batch:** this protocol's own batch (`batch-H-A5-09.md`), Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.05 over its pair p-values. At most 50 pairs.
3. **H-A5-03 is closed to new pairs** (its six registered pairs only; enforced by the runner since PR #61). Any later revision must close H-A5-09 in the same way.
4. **Pairs run under several protocols** are cited by the **least favourable of all their outcomes**, in the order A < B < C (this extends H-A5-03, Part 1, point 4, from two protocols to any number). A C needs C under every protocol the pair was run under.

### Reported besides the decision
As in H-A5-03. In addition, each outcome file reports the control row (real lexicon against the bigram null on Greek words) beside the same row under H-A5-03, as the calibration of the revised null.

**A reading fixed in advance for that calibration** (descriptive, decides no outcome): if, under the bigram null, M1 × English and M1 × Turkish still beat the null on Greek words at p ≤ 0.05, letter co-occurrence is not the explanation of that advantage; if both are above 0.05, it is consistent with being part of it.

### Known-script validation of the revised null (before registration; lexicon side only)
Over 100 draws per lexicon, for M1's three lexicons and M2's three (research-notes 2026-11-18; `docs/benchmark.md`):
- exact counts of expanded forms at every length, as for H-A5-03's null;
- the share of the real lexicon's expanded forms that also occur in a pseudo-lexicon is about the same as under H-A5-03's null (0.38–0.68 against 0.34–0.69), so the bigram null does not copy the real lexicon;
- the total-variation distance between real and pseudo-lexicon bigram distributions falls from 0.14–0.26 (H-A5-03's null) to 0.10–0.13;
- `tests/test_benchmark_inputs.py` checks that every pseudo-form uses only transitions and first letters seen in the real lexicon.
