- **Layer:** L1 (sign-groups read in conventional Linear B values)
- **Type:** exploratory (training split; false-discovery control across the Tier 1 batch)
- **Protocol:** H-A5-01
- **Status:** pre-registered
- **Batch:** Tier 1 batch, pair 2. Pair 1 is H-A5-01 (M1 × Hebrew verbs, outcome A). Running count of A5 hypotheses registered: 2.

Everything not stated here is Part 1 of `hypotheses/H-A5-01.md`, without change: inputs, sign-group definition, 1,000 matched pseudo-lexicons with seed 20261114, both tests, α = 0.05, the decision rule, the batch correction and the descriptive tables.

Code: `src/aegean/benchmark/` as merged in PR #48 (`007c647`). The run is `python -m aegean.benchmark.tier1 --method M1 --lexicon english-verbs --protocol H-A5-02`. The runner applies the batch correction over both pairs (`aegean.benchmark.batch`, PR #47).

## Why this pair
H-A5-01 found M1 uninformative for Hebrew. This pair asks the same question of a language that nobody proposes for Linear A. It is also the first check of what Test 2 assumes: that a lexicon's advantage over pseudo-lexicons, where it has nothing to do with Linear A, is the same size on Linear A and on the Greek control (H-A5-01, "What Test 2 assumes"). An unrelated language is the only kind of lexicon for which any outcome other than A is known in advance to be false.

## Hypothesis
With the English verb lexicon, M1 matches Linear A sign-groups more often than it does with matched pseudo-lexicons (Test 1), and by a wider margin than on Linear B words of known Greek meaning (Test 2).

**Prediction, stated in advance: not C.** English is not related to the language of Linear A by any published view (consensus; `docs/literature-survey-2026-10.md` lists no such proposal), so outcome C would be false.
- **Test 1:** more likely to pass than for Hebrew, possibly at p1 ≤ α. The matched null holds fewer two-letter forms than this lexicon (see "What I already know", null length bias), and two-letter forms are the ones M1 matches most easily. That tilts Test 1 towards English on any input, with no link to Linear A.
- **Test 2:** expected p2 > α. It re-weights the control to Linear A's lengths, so a length-driven advantage should largely cancel. Whether it cancels fully is what this pair checks.
- So outcome A or B with p1 ≤ α only are both consistent with the prediction. The first is more likely if the tilt is small.

**How each outcome will be read** (fixed now, so that it is not chosen after the result):
- **A:** consistent with the protocol behaving as intended for an unrelated language. It does not show that Test 2's assumption holds in general.
- **B with p2 ≤ α, or C:** a false positive of the benchmark for an unrelated language. Recorded as such in the outcome file and in `docs/benchmark.md`; any later C for another lexicon is then not interpretable at Tier 1 without a control that removes this effect. The registered label is still the one the decision rule gives.
- **B with p1 ≤ α only:** the lexicon beats noise on Linear A no more than on Greek. Expected if English differs from pseudo-lexicons in ways unrelated to either input, in particular through the null's shortage of two-letter forms. It would show that Test 1 alone gives false positives and that Test 2 removed them here.

## Data
- **Method:** M1 with the default `M1Rules`, as in H-A5-01. Its licences are those of a Semitic root-matching claim (laryngeals, weak radicals, Semitic affix menus); applying them to English is deliberate. The question is what the method does with an unrelated lexicon, not whether English would be matched by English-specific rules.
- **Lexicon:** `english-verbs` (`aegean.benchmark.lexicons.english_verbs`):
  - single-word, alphabetic verb lemmas of Princeton WordNet 3.0 (`index.verb`, nltk_data commit `550b6625`, WordNet licence), ranked by their tagged-sense count in SemCor (a frequency proxy), ties alphabetical;
  - each verb's first pronunciation in the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (commit `74790861`, BSD-style licence);
  - consonants mapped to M1's classes, by the same groupings as the Hebrew loader: b p f v → P; k g → K; t, th → T; d, dh → D; s z sh zh ch j → S; r l and r-coloured vowels → R; m → M; n ng → N; h → H; w → W; y → J. Vowels dropped;
  - skeletons of 2 to 4 consonants; the first 502 distinct skeletons in rank order (Hebrew's number of skeletons). The 502nd comes from *hurtle*.
  - After M1's lexicon-side interchanges (S as T; D and T either way) the lexicon has 1,015 forms. The matched pseudo-lexicons are drawn to that size, with lengths drawn from this lexicon and its positional letter frequencies. Because repeated forms are redrawn, they end with fewer short forms than the lexicon (see "What I already know"); the protocol is used as registered.
  - **The lexicon is kept as merged in PR #48**, two-consonant skeletons included. Dropping them would make it less like English and would hide the null's length bias rather than test whether Test 2 removes it.
- **Control:** the Linear B words of H-A5-01. English is Indo-European and so distantly related to Greek; it is not "Greek or close to Greek" in the sense of H-A5-01, Part 1, so the control stands. The relationship is too distant for cognates to survive in M1's consonant classes in any number; any effect it has is a structural resemblance (see "What I already know").

## Decision rule
H-A5-01, Part 1, without change. Batch of 2: Benjamini–Hochberg over the pair p-values of H-A5-01 (0.840) and this pair. H-A5-01's outcome is A and cannot change (the correction never changes an A).

**Power check (required).**
- n: 414 distinct eligible sign-groups in the primary input, as in H-A5-01; 902 control words.
- Smallest attainable p, both tests: 1/1001 ≈ 0.001. With two pairs and H-A5-01's pair p at 0.840, this pair's adjusted pair p is min(2 × its raw pair p, 0.840) whenever its raw value is below 0.840. Outcome C therefore needs a raw pair p ≤ 0.025. Attainable.
- The size of effect needed is of the order measured in H-A5-01 (an excess of about 0.03 for Test 1), but the pseudo-lexicons' spread may differ for this lexicon; the outcome file reports it.

## What I already know that could bias this
- **The H-A5-01 result in full** (`hypotheses/outcomes/H-A5-01.md`): Hebrew 0.775 against 0.769 on Linear A (p1 = 0.397); control 0.532 against 0.512 (+0.020; +0.024 at Linear A's lengths); Test 2 difference −0.017 (p2 = 0.840); outcome A. Also its descriptive tables (per source, by length, the Finding 001 null, lexicon size, random sign strings), the Finding 001 addendum, and both reviews.
- **A look at H-A5-01's matches** (2026-11-17, shown in the session, not committed): Hebrew against the first matched pseudo-lexicon on the 414 sign-groups: 287 matched by both, 34 by Hebrew only, 31 by the pseudo-lexicon only, 62 by neither; two-sign groups match under nearly any lexicon.
- **Lexicon-side figures for English** (computed before registration; no hit rate):
  - lengths: 71 two-consonant, 203 three-consonant, 228 four-consonant skeletons (Hebrew: 0, 488, 14);
  - letters: R 0.20, S 0.15, P 0.13, K, N and T 0.11, D 0.09, M 0.05, H and W 0.02, J 0.01;
  - share of forms with adjacent identical letters 0.100 (Hebrew 0.139);
  - 1,015 forms after expansion (Hebrew 664); 103 of the 502 skeletons are also Hebrew skeletons;
  - the ranking skipped 6 verbs without a pronunciation, 217 outside 2–4 consonants and 496 duplicates.
- **Null length bias** (found by the independent review, `docs/reviews/H-A5-02-review.md`, R1; measured over the 1,000 matched pseudo-lexicons of seed 20261114, lexicon side only, no hit rate). `nulls.matched_lexicon` redraws any form already drawn. Short forms collide more often (only 11 × 10 two-letter forms exist at English's letter frequencies), so the pseudo-lexicons end with fewer short and more long forms than the lexicon:

  | Lexicon, form length | Skeletons, real | Skeletons, null mean (95%) | After expansion, real | After expansion, null mean (95%) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | English, 2 | 71 | 50.2 (40–59) | 79 | 61.7 (52–70) |
  | English, 3 | 203 | 174.8 (153–198) | 310 | 301.6 (270–335) |
  | English, 4 | 228 | 231.4 (213–250) | 626 | 651.7 (617–684) |
  | Hebrew, 3 | 488 | 439.4 (413–464) | 634 | 623.4 (601–642) |
  | Hebrew, 4 | 14 | 17.9 (10–25) | 30 | 40.6 (22–63) |

  English has more two-letter forms after expansion than every one of the 1,000 pseudo-lexicons (their maximum is 76). For H-A5-01 the bias ran towards four-letter forms, which M1 rarely matches. That made the null slightly easier to beat, which favoured Hebrew, and outcome A stood. A length-stratified matched null is a candidate for a revised protocol; it is not used here.
- **Expectation, not a result (rule 5):** English will match more sign-groups in absolute terms than Hebrew did, because M1 reads many short sign-groups as two-consonant skeletons and 79 of English's forms have two letters.
- **Not seen:** any hit rate of the English lexicon, or of its pseudo-lexicons, on Linear A sign-groups or on the Greek control.
- **Possible direction of bias, stated in advance:** English and Greek share Indo-European root shapes, and Greek words in Linear B spelling may therefore suit an English lexicon slightly better than Linear A sign-groups do. That would lower D and favour outcome A, so this pair is a weaker check of Test 2 against false positives than a non-Indo-European language would be. A non-Indo-European unrelated language is a candidate for a later pair.
