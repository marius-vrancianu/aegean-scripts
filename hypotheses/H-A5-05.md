## Why this pair
English is Indo-European like Greek, so any resemblance it shares with the Greek control pushes Test 2 towards outcome A (H-A5-02). Turkish (Turkic) is not related to Greek or to the language of Linear A. It is the stricter check of what Test 2 assumes: that a lexicon's advantage over pseudo-lexicons, where it has nothing to do with Linear A, is the same size on Linear A and on the control.

**No published link to Linear A** (subagent search, 2026-11-18, English and Turkish sources; research-notes 2026-11-18): no scholarly proposal found (high confidence); a fringe web claim cannot be excluded (medium confidence). Uralic (Finnish, Hungarian), Basque and Japanese were rejected because Minoan links have been claimed for them.

## Hypothesis
With the Turkish verb lexicon, M1 matches Linear A sign-groups more often than it does with length-stratified matched pseudo-lexicons (Test 1), and by a wider margin than on Linear B words of known Greek meaning (Test 2).

**Prediction, stated in advance: outcome A.** Any outcome with p2 ≤ α is read in advance as a false positive of Test 2 for an unrelated language; B with p1 ≤ α only, as a structural difference between this lexicon and length-matched noise that Test 1 alone cannot handle.

## Data
- **Method:** M1, default rules. Its licences are those of a Semitic root-matching claim, applied to Turkish deliberately, as to English in H-A5-02.
- **Lexicon:** `turkish-verbs` (`aegean.benchmark.lexicons.turkish_verbs`, PR #54):
  - verb entries of the Zemberek-NLP dictionary (commit `fd8c6749`, Apache-2.0): infinitives in -mak/-mek not tagged as another part of speech; 3,220 verbs;
  - light-verb compounds with *etmek*, *olmak* or *eylemek* excluded (133; mostly Arabic and Persian loans; rule `turkish_compound`, based on Zemberek's `Voicing` attribute);
  - ranked by the count of the infinitive in FrequencyWords `tr_full.txt` (OpenSubtitles 2018, commit `525f9b56`, CC BY-SA 4.0), ties alphabetical;
  - the stem's consonants (the infinitive without -mak/-mek) mapped to M1's classes: b p f v → P; k g q → K; t → T; d → D; s z ş c ç j → S; l r → R; m → M; n → N; h → H; y → J; w → W; ğ dropped; vowels dropped;
  - the first 502 distinct skeletons of 2–4 consonants; the 502nd comes from *yankılamak*.
  - 859 forms after M1's interchanges (59, 254 and 546 of two, three and four letters).
- **Control:** as in H-A5-01. Turkish is not Greek or close to it.

## What I already know that could bias this
- Everything listed in H-A5-01 to H-A5-04 and the outcomes and reviews of H-A5-01 and H-A5-02.
- **Lexicon-side figures for Turkish** (no hit rate): skeleton lengths 52 / 169 / 281; letters R 0.29, S 0.15, K 0.11, T 0.10, N 0.09, P 0.08, D and J 0.06, M 0.04, H 0.02; 0.185 of forms have adjacent identical letters (Hebrew 0.139, English 0.100); 91 skeletons shared with Hebrew, 157 with English.
- **Possible biases, stated in advance:**
  - Turkish verb stems keep derivational suffixes, above all the denominal *-la-/-le-*, so many skeletons end in R (hence R at 0.29). Linear A sign-groups and Greek words may suit that differently; this is the kind of unequal non-linguistic advantage Test 2 assumes away, and the pair exists to see whether it does.
  - Verbs built with *-la-* on Arabic nouns (*hesaplamak*) remain in the lexicon, so some Semitic roots are present.
  - Homographs inflate a few frequency counts (*yemek* "to eat" and "food").
- **Seen through the review of PR #55** (`docs/reviews/H-A5-03-review.md`, N1 and N4; lexicon side): only 72 distinct two-letter forms exist after expansion at Turkish's letters, and the stratified pseudo-lexicons share about 52 of the real 59, so at two letters the null is close to a copy and the tests work mainly through three- and four-letter forms. Under H-A5-01's matched null Turkish's two-letter shortfall would have been larger than English's (46.6 on average against the real 59). H-A5-01 takes no new pairs (H-A5-03, Part 1, point 3), so Turkish is run under the revised null only.
- **Not seen:** any hit rate of the Turkish lexicon, or of any pseudo-lexicon for it, on Linear A or on the control.
