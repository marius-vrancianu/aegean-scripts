## Part 1 — Protocol (revision 1 of H-A5-01, Part 1)

**Everything in H-A5-01, Part 1 holds without change, except the decision null and the batch.** That is: terms, data (training split, primary input, per-source rows, the Greek control), both tests, the length bins, p for the pair, α = 0.05, the decision rule (outcomes A, B, C with the batch correction), the deviations rule, the stability and descriptive reporting, and every seed. Where H-A5-01 says "matched pseudo-lexicons", read "length-stratified matched pseudo-lexicons".

### What changes, and why
1. **Decision null: length-stratified matched pseudo-lexicons** (`nulls.stratified_matched_lexicon`). As H-A5-01's matched null, but filled length by length: at every form length a pseudo-lexicon has exactly the real lexicon's number of distinct forms after the method's lexicon-side licences, with letters drawn from the real lexicon's frequencies at that length and position. K = 1,000, drawn in sequence from `random.Random(20261114)`.
   - *Why:* H-A5-01's matched null draws a length per form and redraws repeats. Short forms repeat more often, so it ends with fewer short forms than the real lexicon. For English this left 61.7 two-letter forms on average against the real 79, and Test 1 passed for an unrelated language (p1 = 0.009; H-A5-02 outcome). With the two-letter forms removed from all lexicons, the excess vanished (+0.065 → −0.007). The revised null removes the length difference by construction.
   - It does not remove every structural difference between a real lexicon and its pseudo-lexicons: positional letter frequencies are matched, but letter co-occurrence within a form is not. Test 2 remains for that reason.
2. **Batch:** this protocol's own batch, every pair ever run under H-A5-03, failures included, with Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.05 over its pair p-values (`batch-H-A5-03.md`). H-A5-01's batch is separate and unchanged. The power check of H-A5-01 (at most 50 pairs per batch) applies.

### Known-script validation of the revised null (before registration; lexicon side only)
- Over 200 draws each, the stratified null reproduces the real lexicon's count of expanded forms at every length, exactly, in every draw: Hebrew 634 three- and 30 four-letter forms; English 79, 310 and 626 two-, three- and four-letter forms; Turkish 59, 254 and 546 (`tests/test_benchmark_inputs.py` checks the property on a synthetic lexicon).
- The protocol's own check on real data is pair 2 (H-A5-04): English is unrelated, so Test 1 should no longer pass for it. That run is a check, not a blind test (see H-A5-04).

The re-run measures what the revised null changes for a lexicon of three- and four-letter forms; it adds no new evidence about Hebrew and Linear A.
- **Null length bias for Hebrew under H-A5-01** (lexicon side): 634 three- and 30 four-letter expanded forms, against null means of 623.4 and 40.6. My reasoning, not a measurement: the old null leaned to four-letter forms, which M1 rarely matches, so it was slightly easier to beat. The revised null removes that, so I expect Hebrew's excess to be the same or slightly smaller than +0.006.
