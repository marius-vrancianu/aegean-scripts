- a *complete* group has both edges unbroken and every sign identified.

## Intervals for the headline figures (A1.14 harness)

Seed 11112026, 1000 resamples, 95% percentile intervals, p_doubt 0.5. Point estimate: one source per document, the first available of sigla > lineara.xyz > younger (SigLA first because it marks doubt and sign roles; this order is a convention). *Reading*: one source per document chosen at random, doubtful signs blanked half the time. *Bootstrap*: documents resampled within sites. *Both*: the two together.

How to read this:
- The intervals show how much a figure moves with the choice of source and with the sample of documents. They are not confidence intervals for a property of the language: the surviving documents are not a random sample of what was written.
- The *reading* interval mostly reflects encoding differences between sources (A1.9), not doubt about signs: only 36 signs in the primary reading are marked doubtful.
- **Counts of distinct signs and of signs occurring once fall outside their bootstrap intervals.** That is expected, not an error: a sample drawn with replacement always contains fewer distinct types than the original, so the bootstrap is biased downwards for any count of types. Use it for shares and means, not for inventory sizes.

## Limits

- Training split only. Totals are smaller than any published full-corpus figure by construction.
- The three sources all derive from GORILA, and lineara.xyz from Younger's earlier pages (A1.9). Agreement between columns is not independent confirmation.
- lineara.xyz has no sign roles and incomplete damage marking; its position counts include logograms written inside words and treat unmarked breaks as intact.
- Word division is each source's own. Where sources divide words differently, lengths and positions differ.
- Sign variants are merged by base ID. An analysis that needs variants reads the JSON (`sign_frequency` is by base ID too; variants need a rerun with a different key).
